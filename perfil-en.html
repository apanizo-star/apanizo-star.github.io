Profile

Professional background

Linguistics, lexicography, editing, teaching, and language policy.

Agustín Panizo is a Peruvian linguist, lexicographer, editor, and university lecturer. He holds a Master’s degree in Hispanic Lexicography from the University of León / School of Hispanic Lexicography (2002–2003) and has devoted more than three decades to linguistics and to the study of Quechua and Peru's Indigenous languages.

Education

Master’s degree in Hispanic Lexicography · University of León / School of Hispanic Lexicography · 2002–2003.

Linguistics and Literature, concentration in Hispanic Linguistics · Pontifical Catholic University of Peru · 1992–1999.

His professional career combines linguistic research, the development of dictionaries and lexicographic resources, editing, university teaching, and the design and implementation of public policies related to linguistic diversity.

From 2015 to 2020, he headed the Directorate of Indigenous Languages at Peru's Ministry of Culture, where he contributed to the development of policies on language rights and to initiatives for the documentation and revitalization of Indigenous languages.

He has directed and participated in lexicographic projects and the production of language resources, and has a particular interest in the possibilities offered by open technologies and the Wikimedia ecosystem for language documentation and dissemination.

He currently works in teaching, research, consulting, and editing, with a particular focus on lexicography, language policy and planning, language revitalization, and Quechua teaching.

Alongside this work, since 1994 he has maintained an ongoing activity as a writer and editor in surf-related publications. As a surfer, he has written about the history and culture of Peruvian surfing, its beaches and communities, maritime heritage, and the conservation of surf breaks. He is currently the author of a ten-article series on these topics published periodically in Actualidad Ambiental.
